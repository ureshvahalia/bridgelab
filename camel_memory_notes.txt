Important extracted constants and assumptions
---------------------------------------------

- `1NT` opening in Camel is `11-14`, balanced/semi-balanced, denies a 4-card
  major and shortness.

- `1H` has a special balanced exception:
  balanced `18-20` with `4/5` hearts and no 4 spades.
  This came from `common.tex`:
  - `\onehbalmin = 18`
  - `\onehbalmax = 20`

- `1D` is:
  - balanced `15-23` without a 4-card major, or
  - unbalanced `14+` without a 4-card major.

- `1C` is the most artificial/system-defining opening:
  usually `11+` with a `4/5`-card major and no `6+` major,
  plus rare exceptional no-major hands.

- `2C` and `2D` are limited major-showing openings:
  - `2C` implies hearts
  - `2D` implies spades
  - both are `10-13` and shapely

- `2H` and `2S` are not weak twos in Camel.
  They are balanced/semi-balanced `11-14` with exactly 5 cards in the opened
  major and deny 4 cards in the other major.

- `2N` is a good 7-card-major hand with strong playing-trick content.
  The source defines it by playing tricks rather than HCP, so this had to be
  approximated in DSL.

- `3N` is a forcing opening with a self-sufficient suit and roughly 9+ playing
  tricks. This also had to be approximated.

Implementation notes
--------------------

- Forward references are not allowed in the DSL.
  I initially hit this problem because some "catch-all" sequence rules were
  written as negations of later-defined sequence rules.
  I removed those forward references by replacing them with coarse residual
  hand descriptions.

- The resulting `camel_spec.txt` is intentionally a first-pass uncontested
  system file, not a complete transcription of all relay and competitive logic.

- The file includes:
  - opening bids,
  - major early response families,
  - major early rebid families,
  - summary-level relay entry points for `1N`, `2m`, `2M`, `2N`, `3N`.

- The file does not attempt to encode every competitive chapter, nor all deep
  K.K.-relay continuations.

Interpretation guidelines used
------------------------------

- When the source gave exact shape and control conditions, I encoded them.

- When the source used terms like:
  - "game in hand"
  - "playing tricks"
  - "good suit"
  - "self-sufficient suit"
  - "did not feel like responding 1N"
  I used conservative HCP/shape/honor approximations instead.

- When the source explicitly said "currently undefined", "TBD", or presented
  a clearly judgement-based continuation, I left the deep branch out rather
  than pretending it was precise.
